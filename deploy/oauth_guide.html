Before we begin

What this is, and why we're doing it

Your new booking system needs permission to read and write to your Google Calendar. That way, when a client books a consultation on your website, it automatically appears on your calendar — and when you block off a day for a personal appointment, your website automatically hides that day from your booking calendar.

Google (rightly!) doesn't just hand out that permission. You have to grant it, tied to your own Google account. This guide walks you through the tiny bit of Google setup required — it's a one-time task, takes about five minutes, and is completely free.

Why you have to do this (not your web developer)
If your developer created it under their Google account instead, your booking system would break the moment they deleted the project — and every security email from Google would go to them instead of you. Doing it under your own account keeps you in full control forever.

What you'll end up sending back

At the end of Step 4, Google will show you two values that look something like this:

Client ID:      847293847-abc123xyz.apps.googleusercontent.com
Client Secret:  GOCSPX-aB3cD4eF5gH6iJ7kL8mN9oP0

Copy both, and send them to your developer through a secure channel — a password manager like 1Password, an encrypted message, or a service like Signal. Don't send them in a plain email if you can help it.

Treat these like a house key
Anyone who has both values can talk to Google as though they were you. Once your developer has them, delete them from any chat threads or notes.
1

Create a Google Cloud project

About 60 seconds
  1. Open console.cloud.google.com in your browser.
  2. Sign in with your Gmail (swellballoons@gmail.com).
  3. At the very top of the page, click the project dropdown (it usually says "Select a project"), then click New Project in the top-right of the pop-up.
  4. Give the project a name: Swell design bookings (any name is fine).
  5. Leave the "Organization" field as-is (it's usually blank or "No organization"), and click Create.
  6. Wait about 10 seconds for Google to create the project, then select it from that same top dropdown.
How to tell it worked
Your project name (Swell design bookings) should now appear in the top bar of the console instead of "Select a project".
2

Turn on the Google Calendar service

About 30 seconds
  1. In the search bar at the top of the console, type Calendar API.
  2. In the results, click Google Calendar API.
  3. Click the big blue Enable button.
  4. Wait a few seconds. When the page changes to show a dashboard for the Calendar API, you're done with this step.
3

Fill out the "consent screen"

About 2 minutes

This is the screen your future self will see when clicking "Sign in with Google" — you're telling Google what to display on it.

  1. In the left sidebar, click APIs & Services, then OAuth consent screen. (If prompted "Get Started", click it.)
  2. Choose External as the User Type, then click Create.
  3. Fill out App information:
    • App name: swell design + media
    • User support email: swellballoons@gmail.com
    • App logo: optional (skip if you don't have a square PNG handy)
  4. Fill out App domain (all fields are optional, but nicer for the consent screen):
    • Application home page: https://swelldesignla.com
    • Privacy policy: https://swelldesignla.com/privacy
    • Terms of service: https://swelldesignla.com/terms
  5. Under Authorized domains, click "+ Add domain" and enter swelldesignla.com.
  6. Developer contact: swellballoons@gmail.com.
  7. Click Save and continue.
  8. On the Scopes page, click Add or remove scopes. Search for and tick each of:
    • .../auth/calendar.events
    • .../auth/calendar.readonly
    • .../auth/userinfo.email
    • .../auth/userinfo.profile
    Click Update, then Save and continue.
  9. On the Test users page, click + Add users and add swellballoons@gmail.com (and any teammate's Gmail if you want them to be able to connect too).
  10. Click Save and continue, then Back to dashboard.
Leave "Publishing status" as Testing
You do NOT need Google verification. Keeping it in "Testing" mode is intentional — it means only the emails you list as Test Users can connect. That's perfect for a single-designer site, and it saves you a ~2 week Google review process. You can list up to 100 test users, so you'll never run out.
4

Create your OAuth Client ID

About 60 seconds — this is where the two values you'll send back come from
  1. In the left sidebar, click APIs & Services, then Credentials.
  2. At the top, click + Create Credentials, then OAuth client ID.
  3. Set Application type to Web application.
  4. Set Name to Swell backend.
  5. Under Authorized redirect URIs, click + Add URI and paste each of the following on a separate line (add all three — takes 15 seconds and prevents future headaches):
    • https://swelldesignla.com/api/integrations/google/callback
    • https://www.swelldesignla.com/api/integrations/google/callback
    • https://balloon-decor-cms.preview.emergentagent.com/api/integrations/google/callback
  6. Click the blue Create button.
  7. A pop-up will appear showing your Client ID and Client Secret. This is what you need to send back.

Reference

Three things on that screen — and what each one is

You'll see three "things" on the Create OAuth client screen. It's easy to confuse them, so here's what each one actually is:

Name Example Who creates it Where it goes
Redirect URI https://swelldesignla.com/api/integrations/google/callback You type it in (it's your website's return address) Stays inside Google
Client ID 847293847-abc123xyz.apps.googleusercontent.com Google generates it for you Copy & send to your developer
Client Secret GOCSPX-aB3cD4eF5gH6iJ7kL8mN9oP0 Google generates it for you Copy & send to your developer
A helpful analogy
Think of it like setting up mail forwarding at the post office. The Redirect URI is your home address — you tell Google where to send things. The Client ID is your account number — Google gives it to you so it knows who's asking. The Client Secret is your PIN — Google gives it to you, and you keep it private.

Why the Redirect URI has to be exact

Google will only send visitors back to a URL that exactly matches one you registered. Even tiny differences count as a mismatch:

URL Match? Why
https://swelldesignla.com/api/integrations/google/callback ✓ The reference
https://www.swelldesignla.com/api/integrations/google/callback ✗ Extra "www."
http://swelldesignla.com/api/integrations/google/callback ✗ http vs https
https://swelldesignla.com/api/integrations/google/callback/ ✗ Trailing slash
https://SwellDesignLA.com/api/integrations/google/callback ✗ Uppercase letters

That's why the guide asks you to add three variations — with www., without www., and the preview URL. It covers every place your site might send visitors back from.

What happens after you send the values

Your one-time click, forever

Once your developer has the two values, they'll paste them into the server that runs your website (about 30 seconds on their side). Then you'll do this one time, and then never again:

  1. Log into your admin dashboard on swelldesignla.com/admin.
  2. Go to Integrations in the sidebar.
  3. Under "Google Calendar" you'll see a big button: Sign in with Google. Click it.
  4. Google will show you a familiar sign-in screen. Pick swellballoons@gmail.com, click Allow.
  5. You'll be sent back to the admin dashboard, and it'll say Connected as swellballoons@gmail.com.
That's it — the setup is done forever
From this moment on, every consultation your clients book on the website will automatically appear on your Google Calendar, and any personal events on your calendar will automatically block those times from being booked on the website. No manual work, no double-bookings, no back-and-forth.
If something goes wrong

Troubleshooting

"Access blocked: swell design has not completed the Google verification process"

Your Gmail isn't in the Test Users list yet. Go back to Step 3, item 9 and add swellballoons@gmail.com as a Test User.

"Error 400: redirect_uri_mismatch"

The URL your browser is trying to go back to doesn't exactly match any of the ones you registered in Step 4, item 5. The error page will show which URL was attempted — copy it, and add it to the list of Authorized redirect URIs in Google Cloud.

"invalid_client"

The Client ID or Client Secret your developer entered on the server doesn't match what Google shows. Ask them to double-check by comparing to what's shown in your Google Cloud Console (Credentials → click the OAuth client you created).

"Nothing happens when I click Sign in with Google"

Your developer needs to check the server logs. Usually this means the credentials weren't picked up. They'll know what to do.

Where to get the values again if you lose them

  1. Go to console.cloud.google.com and select your Swell design bookings project.
  2. Sidebar → APIs & Services → Credentials.
  3. Click the OAuth client named Swell backend.
  4. The Client ID is shown on-screen. To see the Client Secret again, click "Reset secret" (this will invalidate the old one — only do this if you truly lost it, and let your developer know so they can update the server).
The finish line

Ready to send your developer?

Before you send those two values, do a quick sanity check:

  • ☐ You completed all four steps.
  • ☐ Your Gmail is listed under Test Users (Step 3, item 9).
  • ☐ You added at least the two URIs with and without www. (Step 4, item 5).
  • ☐ You copied the Client ID — it ends in .apps.googleusercontent.com
  • ☐ You copied the Client Secret — it starts with GOCSPX-
  • ☐ You're sending both values through a secure channel (password manager, encrypted message), not plain email.
